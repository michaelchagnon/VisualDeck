Video cues for live shows, on Windows.

VisualDeck is free, open-source software that plays video, images and live cameras on projectors and screens, cue by cue. Build the show as a grid of cues and layers, choose which screen each layer goes to, and press GO.

Build the show as a grid

Each column is a cue and each row is a layer. Drag clips and stills from the media list into the cells, and give each cue the number the stage manager calls (5.5 is fine) and a name you'll recognize at a glance.

Lower rows draw over higher ones, so a title, a rain overlay or a logo is just another layer. Notes in Cue Settings appear above the preview while the cue is live.

  • Transitions for each cue: cut, fade, smooth fade or dip to black.
  • Follows: carry on after a set time, or when the cue's videos end.
  • Undo and redo for every change to the show.

Run it with GO

Space is GO, Shift+Space is BACK and Ctrl+B fades to black. Click a cue's header to cut straight to it. The next cue is already loaded, so cuts land on time; mark any other cue Keep Ready and it will too.

You can change the cue that's on screen without the audience seeing it. Edits wait in a second preview until you press Update Output. For rehearsals, Start Cue From drops you into the middle of a cue.

Before the house opens, Preshow Check lists missing media, a wrong output, a camera that won't open or a remote that isn't listening. Lock Editing then keeps the grid from changing by accident, while GO, BACK and Blackout still work.

Fit the picture to the room

Corner pin each display to square up a projector that hits the screen at an angle, or switch on mesh warp to bend the picture over a curved or uneven surface. Drag a point, or nudge it a pixel at a time with the arrow keys.

Edge blending and Span Displays join two or more projectors into one wide picture, side by side or in a grid, and slices let you place each projector's piece by hand. Test patterns (grid, color bars, white) show what's really on the wall, and output presets keep the setup for each venue.

Layers, looks and effects

Every cell has its own position, size and rotation, opacity, blend mode, color correction and a soft-edged rectangle or ellipse mask. Warp points bend a single picture to fit a piece of scenery.

Up to four real-time shader effects can sit on each layer and on each display. Nine come with VisualDeck, and you can add your own in the ISF format. Effects and blend modes need Performance Mode, which draws with the graphics card.

Each clip can also play faster or slower, in reverse or back and forth, between in and out points. If a file is too heavy to play smoothly, Make Playback Copy converts it to one that starts at once.

Take cues from the desk, or from a phone

VisualDeck can follow the rest of the show. Each connection is off until you turn it on in Preferences › Remote control, and OSC and the browser remote accept only this computer until you allow the network.

ConnectionWhat it can do
OSCGO, BACK, Blackout, Update Output, go to a cue by number or name, start a cue partway in, and set effect values. Any OSC sender can use it: QLab, Bitfocus Companion, TouchOSC and others.
Browser remoteA page for any phone or tablet on the network, with GO, BACK, Blackout and the cue list. The same commands are open to scripts as an HTTP API.
MIDIKeys or buttons for GO, BACK, Blackout and Update Output and faders for effect values, set up with MIDI learn. Program Change chooses a cue.
MIDI Show ControlGO, with or without a cue number, and STOP (a fade to black) from a lighting or sound desk.
TimecodeMIDI timecode or SMPTE LTC from an audio input: each cue runs when the timecode reaches its time.
Art-Net and sACNChoose cues, GO and Blackout from a lighting console, and send the live cue's number back to it.

Show-control connections have been tested with messages built to each standard, not yet with every console and device. Known issues has the details.

Made to run all night

Output Health shows the frame rate, late frames, every decoder and the sound, and says in plain words what's wrong when something is. VisualDeck also notices a video that has stopped moving. Shows are autosaved every two minutes to a separate file, never over the show, and can be recovered after a crash. Missing media can be relinked, and Collect Show copies a show and all its media into one folder for the venue.

For installations, Presentation Mode loops the cues by itself, the schedule runs cues at set times of day, and the computer doesn't go to sleep while VisualDeck is open. Before a long run, the built-in Soak Test plays your show unattended for as long as you choose and reports anything that grew or failed. A VisualDeck 1.0 release candidate ran in that test for 72 hours with no render errors.

Formats and specifications

VideoH.264, H.265 (HEVC), ProRes including 4444 with transparency, HAP, HAP Alpha and DXV, in MP4, MOV, AVI and MKV files
ImagesJPG, PNG with transparency, BMP and GIF
Live sourcesUSB cameras and capture cards; network streams such as UDP and RTSP, with sound and automatic reconnection
SoundFrom video files, to the output device you choose, with a volume for each video and a delay to line sound up with the projector
PlaybackSpeed, reverse, ping-pong, in and out points; Make Playback Copy for hard-to-play files
OutputsAny number of displays, with each layer sent to the display you choose; corner pin, mesh warp, edge blending, spans, slices, test patterns and output presets
EffectsNine built-in ISF shader effects plus your own; up to four on each layer and on each display
Show controlOSC, browser remote and HTTP API, MIDI, MIDI Show Control, MIDI timecode, SMPTE LTC, Art-Net and sACN in and out
Render modesPerformance (GPU, OpenGL 3.3), Balanced and Compatibility (CPU)
SystemWindows 10 or 11, 64-bit. Performance Mode needs a graphics card or integrated GPU with OpenGL 3.3. 8 GB of memory recommended.
LicenseMozilla Public License 2.0. The installer includes Python, FFmpeg (an LGPL build) and every library VisualDeck uses.
Fit

Who it's for

  • Theater and dance: video cues called by the stage manager, with GO, BACK, follows and a locked show file.
  • Concerts, conferences and events: backgrounds, walk-in loops, sponsor reels and camera feeds across several screens.
  • Museums, exhibitions and installations: looping playback that runs by itself for days.
  • VJs and live visuals: layers, blend modes and shader effects you can play from MIDI or OSC.

What it doesn't do (yet)

  • Audio-only or lighting cues. VisualDeck plays picture, and the sound that comes with it.
  • NDI, Spout or Syphon, 3D projection mapping, or a timeline.
  • macOS. The release is for Windows; the source also runs on Linux.
